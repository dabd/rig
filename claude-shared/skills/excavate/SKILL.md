# Excavate

Map the assumptions that make the supplied claim or plan coherent. Separate
compound claims when their dependencies differ. Follow only branches that
matter to understanding, uncertainty, or a decision.

Distinguish empirical, normative, structural, psychological, and definitional
assumptions where the distinction changes how they can be examined. Label
inferences about people as hypotheses, not established motives.

Mark a crux when changing it would materially change the conclusion.
Distinguish uncertainty from importance, and identify assumptions whose
resolution would settle several branches.

Deliver a compact dependency tree or connected explanation, the decisive
cruxes, and concrete probes that could update them. Recurse until reaching a
useful explanatory boundary, a value premise, or redundancy. No minimum count
or fixed depth is required. Do not manufacture layers or turn diagnosis into
an unsolicited opposing argument.
