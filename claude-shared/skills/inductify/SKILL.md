# Inductify

Use multiple distinct examples to infer a shared mechanism or constraint.
Judge input adequacy by the evidence available, not sentence or paragraph
counts. A small sample can support a tentative hypothesis; distinguish it
from a robust generalization. With one case, explain the limitation and
analyze it without claiming cross-case induction.

Compare each case's mechanism, assumptions, values or optimization targets,
constraints, and outcome. Consider recurring evidence structures, behaviors,
and timing where they explain the cases; induction is not limited to one
causal pattern type.
Prioritize similarities that explain behavior or predict something beyond
the supplied cases. Surface differences and counterexamples that could
invalidate the proposed pattern.

Check the relevant base rate and actively consider competing patterns. Note
expected similarities that are absent. Near-duplicate or selectively chosen
cases provide little independent support; do not mistake a shared source for
replication. If a mechanism remains unknown, label it instead of inventing one.

For each supported pattern, state:

- What recurs and the evidence in each case.
- Why it might recur, separating causal evidence from analogy.
- A prediction for another case and what would falsify it.
- Its scope and the uncertainty introduced by sampling or selection.

Acknowledge obvious overlap only when it helps explain a deeper distinction.
Group related patterns if useful. No fixed finding count, scoring rubric,
or invocation of other skills is required. Recommend the most informative
next observation when the user needs greater confidence.
