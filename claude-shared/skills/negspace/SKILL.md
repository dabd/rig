# Negspace

Find missing premises, conclusions, or alternatives whose absence changes how
a reader should interpret the supplied text.

Ground each candidate in a quoted passage, a logical dependency, or a concrete
expectation of the document's genre. Explain what is absent and why it matters.
Distinguish a premise required for the argument from an optional continuation
that merely seems plausible.

Consider benign explanations: brevity, scope, shared context, or unavailable
information. An omission alone does not establish concealment or intent.
Status protection, withheld ambition, shared assumptions, blind spots, and
preserved optionality are possible interpretations, not facts about the author.

Report only supported candidates, including none. For each, give the textual
anchor, missing content, consequence, and uncertainty or alternative explanation.
Use omission categories only when they add explanatory value.

This is qualitative textual inference. Do not claim measured perplexity spikes,
next-sentence probabilities, or knowledge of hidden motives. Do not infer that
a factual text is complete solely because its tone is direct.
