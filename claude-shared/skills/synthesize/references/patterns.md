# Patterns for difficult syntheses

Choose a pattern only when it explains the evidence. These are alternatives,
not stages to run in sequence.

- Regime partition: claims hold under different conditions. Identify the
  condition and evidence for its boundary. For example, independent tasks and
  tightly coupled tasks may respond differently to a work arrangement.
- Causal chain: positions describe different links or opposing feedbacks in
  one process. Trace both directions; do not assume that correlation establishes
  either arrow.
- Level mismatch: individual behavior and aggregate outcomes can differ.
  Specify how one produces the other and where the explanation stops.
- Hidden variable: claims condition on an unstated difference. Name an
  observation that would distinguish this explanation from a convenient story.

For more than two positions, compare their cruxes first. Cluster compatible
claims or map the dimensions of disagreement. Pairwise synthesis can discard
qualifications early, so compare the final account with every original input.

A worked explanation can show the question, competing predictions, proposed
mechanism, conditions of applicability, and a distinguishing observation.
Numerical examples must identify whether their values are measured, sourced,
or hypothetical. Do not manufacture policy thresholds for abstract qualities.

Value conflicts may remain tradeoffs. Preserve them explicitly instead of
presenting a common factual account as agreement on priorities.
