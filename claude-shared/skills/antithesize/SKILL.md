# Antithesize

Write a coherent alternative position that a reader can understand without
seeing the original. Match the genre and question of the input: an alternative
recommendation for a recommendation, or an alternative mechanism for an
explanation.

Identify the strongest defensible version of the original and its decisive
assumption. Keep established facts and actual constraints fixed. Change the
interpretation, mechanism, or value priority that produces the conclusion;
do not invent facts to sustain opposition.

The deliverable leads with the rival thesis and its positive account of how
things work. Add the crux separating the positions and evidence that could
adjudicate it when useful. Distinguish an exploratory alternative from an
endorsed factual claim. If no credible rival fits the evidence, say so.

Choose the depth and tone from the user's request. Do not require two axes,
a declared intensity setting, or a preliminary approval ceremony.

If the user instead requests falsification, robustness testing, or a broader
menu of opposition methods, use [opposition.md](references/opposition.md).
Those modes have their own deliverables; a counterexample need not pretend
to be a complete worldview.
